Who Were the Signers

Famous Signers

Some of the most famous signers of the Declaration of Independence include

Thomas Jefferson
Main author of the Declaration of Independence and later the 3rd U.S. president.
John Hancock
President of the Continental Congress, famous for his large and bold signature.
Benjamin Franklin
Scientist, inventor, diplomat, and one of the most influential Founding Fathers.
John Adams
Strong supporter of independence and later the 2nd U.S. president.
Samuel Adams
Revolutionary leader and cousin of John Adams who strongly supported American independence.
George Washington
Major leader of the American Revolution, but he did not sign the Declaration because he was serving as commander of the Continental Army.
James Madison
Famous Founding Father who later became the 4th U.S. president, but he did not sign the Declaration because he was not a member of the Continental Congress at the time.

There were 56 total signers, representing the 13 colonies.

Signers by State

New Hampshire

  • Josiah Bartlett — Physician and political leader who became the first delegate from New Hampshire to vote for independence.
  • William Whipple — Merchant and military officer who served as a brigadier general during the Revolutionary War.

Massachusetts

  • John Hancock — President of the Continental Congress, famous for his large and prominent signature.
  • John Adams — Revolutionary leader and future second president of the United States who strongly advocated independence.
  • Samuel Adams — Revolutionary politician and influential organizer of resistance to British rule.
  • Robert Treat Paine — Lawyer and politician who later became Massachusetts' first attorney general.

Rhode Island

  • William Ellery — Merchant and politician who served as a delegate to the Continental Congress.
  • Stephen Hopkins — Experienced colonial politician and former governor of Rhode Island.

Connecticut

  • Samuel Huntington — Politician who later served as president of the Continental Congress.
  • Roger Sherman — Lawyer and politician who signed the Continental Association, Declaration of Independence, Articles of Confederation, and U.S. Constitution.
  • William Williams — Merchant and politician who served in the Connecticut legislature.
  • Oliver Wolcott — Physician, politician, and military officer who supported the Revolutionary cause.

New York

  • William Floyd — Politician and landowner who later served in the first U.S. Congress.
  • Philip Livingston — Merchant and politician from a prominent New York family.
  • Francis Lewis — Merchant and politician whose home and property were destroyed by British forces during the Revolution.
  • Lewis Morris — Landowner and politician who served in the Continental Congress.

New Jersey

  • Richard Stockton — Lawyer and judge who was captured and imprisoned by the British during the Revolutionary War.
  • John Witherspoon — Minister, educator, and president of the College of New Jersey, now Princeton University.
  • Francis Hopkinson — Lawyer, poet, musician, and political satirist.
  • John Hart — Farmer and politician who later served as president of New Jersey's Provincial Congress.
  • Abraham Clark — Lawyer and politician who served multiple terms in the Continental Congress.

Pennsylvania

  • Robert Morris — Merchant and financier who helped finance the American Revolution.
  • Benjamin Rush — Physician, educator, and prominent advocate for independence.
  • Benjamin Franklin — Scientist, inventor, diplomat, and influential Founding Father.
  • John Morton — Politician who helped Pennsylvania's delegation support independence.
  • George Clymer — Merchant and politician who later became a member of the first U.S. Congress.
  • James Smith — Lawyer and political leader who served in the Continental Congress.
  • George Taylor — Iron manufacturer and politician who represented Pennsylvania in Congress.
  • James Wilson — Lawyer and political thinker who later became a justice of the U.S. Supreme Court.
  • George Ross — Lawyer and politician who served in the Continental Congress.

Delaware

  • Caesar Rodney — Political and military leader who famously rode through the night to cast Delaware's deciding vote for independence.
  • George Read — Lawyer and politician who later became a U.S. senator and Chief Justice of Delaware.
  • Thomas McKean — Lawyer and politician who later served as governor of Pennsylvania.

Maryland

  • Samuel Chase — Lawyer and politician who later became an associate justice of the U.S. Supreme Court.
  • William Paca — Lawyer and politician who later became governor of Maryland.
  • Thomas Stone — Lawyer and politician who served on the committee that drafted the Articles of Confederation.
  • Charles Carroll of Carrollton — Wealthy planter and politician who was the last surviving signer of the Declaration.

Virginia

  • George Wythe — Lawyer, scholar, and teacher who mentored future leaders such as Thomas Jefferson.
  • Richard Henry Lee — Politician who introduced the resolution calling for American independence.
  • Thomas Jefferson — Primary author of the Declaration of Independence and later the third U.S. president.
  • Benjamin Harrison — Wealthy planter and politician who served as president of the Continental Congress.
  • Thomas Nelson Jr. — Politician and military leader who later served as governor of Virginia.
  • Francis Lightfoot Lee — Virginia planter and politician who served in the Continental Congress.
  • Carter Braxton — Wealthy planter and merchant who represented Virginia in the Continental Congress.

North Carolina

  • William Hooper — Lawyer and politician who represented North Carolina in the Continental Congress.
  • Joseph Hewes — Merchant and politician who served as a North Carolina delegate to Congress.
  • John Penn — Lawyer and politician who represented North Carolina in the Continental Congress.

South Carolina

  • Edward Rutledge — Lawyer and politician who was one of the youngest delegates to sign the Declaration.
  • Thomas Heyward Jr. — Lawyer, judge, and planter who served in the Continental Congress.
  • Thomas Lynch Jr. — Politician and planter who signed the Declaration despite suffering from serious health problems.
  • Arthur Middleton — Wealthy planter and politician who served in the Continental Congress.

Georgia

  • Button Gwinnett — Politician and planter who served as president of Georgia's revolutionary council.
  • Lyman Hall — Physician and politician who later became governor of Georgia.
  • George Walton — Lawyer and politician who later served as governor of Georgia.
